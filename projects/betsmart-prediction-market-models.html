In Short

The problem

Prediction market prices already reflect most public information. A model only adds value where its probability is both different from the market's and more accurate, and that is hard to prove.

What I built

Three models, for WNBA winners, WNBA points spreads and MLB winners. Each prices every game, compares it with the market, and flags only the contracts where the gap is large enough to matter.

The result

3,600+ trades across nine seasons of testing, all three models profitable, including on the final seasons that were kept sealed until the models were finished.

How It Works

Key Decisions

Judge models on return, not accuracy

Across 27 candidate models, accuracy (AUC) had almost no link to profit. Every change had to improve out-of-sample return to ship.

Trade-off: slower iteration, since each idea needs a full backtest.

Walk-forward, with sealed seasons

Each season is predicted only by a model trained on earlier ones, and the final seasons stay untouched until the end.

Trade-off: less data to tune on, in exchange for honest results.

Agreement as the filter (MLB)

Two models must both see value on the same side. Alone they returned +1.4% and −4.2% on sealed seasons; together, +7.3%.

Trade-off: fewer trades, only about one game in four.

Simple models where the signal is weak

For spreads, a regularised linear model beat LightGBM, and position sizes use a cautious fraction of Kelly to keep drawdowns survivable.

Trade-off: smaller positions mean slower growth in good seasons.

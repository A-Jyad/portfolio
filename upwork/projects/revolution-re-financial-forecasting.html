In Short

The problem

Revolution RE's clients wanted to know how a multifamily property should perform: its expected rent, expenses and profit, and how far reality could stray from that, without a lengthy analysis.

What I built

A model built on data from more than 1,000 US apartment buildings and tested on 441 of them. A client enters their property's characteristics, such as unit count, unit size and age, and gets every income and expense line, up to NOI and cash flow.

The result

Clients get an estimate on demand, and each one comes with a 95% range that was checked against what real properties actually earned.

Tested Against Reality

A forecast range is only useful if it is honest. I tested the model on 441 properties and 11,067 months of real results. A trustworthy 95% range should contain the actual figure about 95% of the time, and the key P&L lines land right on that target:

How It Works

Key Decisions

Anchor everything on market rent

Rent drives most other lines, so it is modelled first with a mixed effects model and fed into every other model.

Trade-off: errors in rent carry downstream, so the ranges account for them.

The right model for each metric

Common lines, rare lines and annual bills such as taxes each get the model type that suits them.

Trade-off: three model families to maintain instead of one.

Build totals from the lines

NOI and cash flow are calculated from the forecast lines, and the downside case pairs low income with high costs.

Trade-off: ranges on totals are deliberately conservative.

Judge the ranges, not just the fit

Success meant the actual figure landing inside the 95% range about 95% of the time, not a high accuracy score.

Trade-off: rare, one-off items are flagged as directional only.
